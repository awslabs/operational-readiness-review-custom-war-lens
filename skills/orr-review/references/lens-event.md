How to score one question:

1. Selected means the statements that are true today and backed by evidence.
2. If None of these (`none_no`) is selected, alone or with other statements, the question scores its maximum risk.
3. Otherwise go through the risk lines in order; the first line that is true gives the risk. A statement id is true when it is selected. AND needs both sides, OR needs at least one side, NOT turns true into false, and parentheses group. "otherwise" is always true.
4. A question marked not applicable as a whole, with a reason, has no risk level: report it as Not applicable. A question not answered yet is Unanswered, never No risk.

## 01 - Prepare

### `evt_calendar_freeze`: Event calendar, decision owner and change freeze

Maximum risk: High. Category: Deployment safety. Core path: no.

Question: Before a scheduled peak, are the event dates, the accountable owner, the go/no-go criteria and the change controls for the event window fixed and written down? The final-review statement stays unselected until that review is done in the final two weeks.

Evidence to collect: the event plan with its dates, partner and dependency dates and success criteria. The named owner, deputy and go/no-go criteria. The deployment blocker configuration for the freeze and the event window, and its emergency path. Notes from the final two-week review.

Statements:

- `cf_dates` (core): The event window, key dates (including partner and dependency dates) and success criteria are documented
- `cf_owner` (core): A named event owner and go/no-go criteria for the event are documented
- `cf_freeze` (secondary): Deployments are blocked automatically during the freeze and the event window, with a logged emergency path for urgent fixes
- `cf_final_review` (secondary): In the final two weeks, open High risks, security findings, edge rules and privileged access were re-reviewed
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `cf_dates` AND `cf_owner` AND `cf_freeze` AND `cf_final_review`
2. Medium: `cf_dates` AND `cf_owner`
3. High: otherwise

### `evt_peak_retest`: Event-specific load re-test

Maximum risk: High. Category: Defense against overload. Core path: no.

Question: Has capacity been re-proven against this event's own forecast, recently enough that later changes cannot have invalidated the result? The re-test statement stays unselected until a re-test has run 30-60 days before the event.

Evidence to collect: the agreed event forecast (peak, shape, ramp, duration, worst case and margin). The re-test plan and report with its date, achieved rate, latency, errors and first bottleneck. The alarm, scaling and runbook observations recorded during the test.

Statements:

- `pr_forecast` (core): The event's peak, shape, duration, ramp and worst case are agreed with the business owner
- `pr_retested` (core): The load test was re-run against this event's forecast plus margin within 30-60 days of the event
- `pr_alarms_validated` (secondary): Alarms, scaling and runbooks were validated during the re-test, and the results were recorded
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `pr_forecast` AND `pr_retested` AND `pr_alarms_validated`
2. Medium: `pr_forecast` AND `pr_retested`
3. High: otherwise

### `evt_capacity_quotas`: Quotas and capacity secured for the event dates

Maximum risk: High. Category: Defense against overload. Core path: no.

Question: Are the quotas and capacity that the event needs approved and in place for the event dates, with enough lead time, and is there a plan to scale back down afterward?

Evidence to collect: applied quota values or approved increase requests for each account and Region, including recovery Regions and third-party APIs. Capacity reservations, scheduled scaling or pre-scaling settings for the event dates. The post-event scale-down and cost plan.

Statements:

- `cq_quotas_raised` (core): Quota increases for peak plus failover were approved ahead of the event, without relying on automatic adjustment
- `cq_capacity_secured` (secondary): The capacity for the opening peak is already running (pre-scaled) before the window, not launched at the peak
- `cq_scale_down_plan` (secondary): A post-event scale-down and cost plan exists
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `cq_quotas_raised` AND `cq_capacity_secured` AND `cq_scale_down_plan`
2. Medium: `cq_quotas_raised`
3. High: otherwise

### `evt_ddos_response`: DDoS response readiness for high-visibility endpoints

Maximum risk: High. Category: Defense against overload. Core path: no.

Question: Are the event's internet-facing endpoints ready to absorb and respond to network and application-layer DDoS attacks during a high-visibility event? A third-party web application firewall or DDoS protection service in front of an endpoint meets these statements when it has the same evidence. For endpoints that a SaaS provider hosts, the provider's evidence meets these statements, as for any partner. Record the provider's protection and escalation contact.

Out of scope if: the workload has no internet-facing endpoints. Select the first statement and keep its evidence.

Evidence to collect: the list of internet-facing endpoints and the web ACL in front of each. The application-layer DDoS mechanism, its mode, sensitivity and alarms. The IaC diff showing the web ACL reconciled. The DDoS tier decision. The DDoS runbook and the date and notes of its last rehearsal.

Statements:

- `dd_not_public` (scope exit): The workload has no internet-facing endpoints
- `dd_l7_enforcing` (core): Application-layer DDoS rules were tuned in Count (monitor-only) mode, are enforcing before the event, and match IaC
- `dd_ddos_tier` (secondary): Shield Advanced with SRT access and proactive engagement is configured, or a documented decision explains why not
- `dd_rehearsed` (secondary): The DDoS runbook was rehearsed, including how to engage AWS and, if subscribed, how to request cost protection
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `dd_not_public` OR (`dd_l7_enforcing` AND `dd_ddos_tier` AND `dd_rehearsed`)
2. Medium: `dd_l7_enforcing`
3. High: otherwise

Accept the scope exit `dd_not_public` only with this evidence: No component of the workload accepts traffic from the internet: there are no public load balancers, API endpoints, CDN distributions, public IP addresses or public DNS names, and users reach it only through private networks. Evidence to collect: an inventory from your IaC or network design showing no internet-facing endpoints, and the control that prevents new ones, for example an organization guardrail. If any endpoint used during the event is internet-facing, do not select this.

### `evt_rehearsal`: Event rehearsal

Maximum risk: High. Category: Escalation. Core path: no.

Question: Has the team rehearsed the failure scenarios most likely to hurt this event, close enough to the event that the people, runbooks and contacts are current? The 60-day statements stay unselected until each exercise has run inside that window. Re-answer them at the 4-week and 2-week checkpoints.

Evidence to collect: the rehearsal plan and scenarios. The dates, attendees and notes of the tabletop and the game day. Detection and mitigation times. Findings with owners and their closure or acceptance.

Statements:

- `rh_tabletop` (alternative A): An event-scenario tabletop (DDoS, AZ impairment, dependency outage, surge beyond forecast) ran within 60 days
- `rh_gameday` (alternative A): A game day under event-like load ran within 60 days
- `rh_findings_closed` (secondary): Rehearsal findings were closed or formally accepted before the event
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `rh_tabletop` AND `rh_gameday` AND `rh_findings_closed`
2. Medium: `rh_tabletop` OR `rh_gameday`
3. High: otherwise

Maximum risk: High. Category: Escalation. Core path: no.

Question: Is the event window staffed with people who can act, and can they reach every team, partner and provider they depend on quickly, through more than one channel? Risk:

3. High: otherwise

### `evt_degraded_modes`: Tested degraded modes and public communications

Maximum risk: High. Category: Blast radius containment. Core path: no.

Question: If demand or a dependency failure exceeds what the workload can serve during the event, can you shed load in tested ways and keep customers and stakeholders informed? A degraded mode is a deliberate reduced service, for example a waiting room, a read-only or static version, or accepting requests for later processing, instead of errors. It is not an emergency code fix.

Evidence to collect: the degraded modes and feature switches, with the date each was last tested under load and how long it took to engage. The pre-approved status messages, approvers and stakeholder notification list.

Statements:

- `dm_degraded_mode` (alternative A): A waiting room or visitor prioritization, static fallback, read-only or async-intake mode was tested
- `dm_kill_switches` (alternative A): Non-essential features can be shed within minutes without a deployment
- `dm_status_comms` (secondary): Pre-approved public status messages and a stakeholder notification list are ready for the event window
- `none_no`: None of these

Risk:

1. No risk: `dm_degraded_mode` AND `dm_kill_switches` AND `dm_status_comms`
2. Medium: `dm_degraded_mode` OR `dm_kill_switches`
3. High: otherwise
